GALLERY: A C E

I compared the current renders of A, C and E (m5, m1, watch, place2) against the 39 reference shots at full size, judged as store screenshots: flavour, clarity, polish. All three belong in the gallery. A is one of the better ones in its group; C and E are about average.

## A. The Desk -- better than average; belongs here: yes
References:
- `diegetic/shots/not-for-broadcast-1.jpg` and `not-for-broadcast-2.jpg`: the same job, a desk of hardware readouts framing one main screen. A's brass P(Doom) dial, needle reputation meter and amber counters are at that level of craft.
- `diegetic/shots/papers-please-1.jpg`: the desk-as-screen register. A's slips and folders on a rail do the same job with clearer costs.
- `reference-games/shots/suzerain-1.jpg`: a desk and documents in a turn-based planning game; A is as clear and warmer.
- `diegetic/shots/frostpunk-1.jpg`: one dominant instrument for the number that kills you. A's dial reads as strongly.

Of the three, A comes closest to "an office with a bunch of computers with Doom approaching". Its WATCH view (operator facing the office, an event slip on the desk) is the strongest single image of ours in the gallery.

What holds it back:
1. In PLAN, the slip rack reads as a flat grid of identical cards rather than objects on a desk. Papers, Please and Not For Broadcast vary the shape and depth of their objects.
2. In PLAN, the office shows only at the edges.

## C. The Paperwork Desk -- about average (upper half); belongs here: yes
References:
- `diegetic/shots/papers-please-1.jpg` and `papers-please-2.jpg`: the same paperwork register. C's form, stamps and end-of-month ledger slip (place2) are a fair match for the Papers, Please day-end sheet.
- `diegetic/shots/orwell-1.jpg`: a document dossier with highlighted facts. C's memo with a portrait and a highlighted "reduced cost" matches it.
- `diegetic/shots/shenzhen-io-2.jpg`: printed paper as the interface. C's Form A-20 has the same printed-sheet authority.
- `reference-games/shots/suzerain-1.jpg`: document-driven turn planning.

C is the clearest of the three to read, and its WATCH and ledger views are polished.

What holds it back:
1. The PLAN card file is a large dark-brown grid of near-identical manila tiles. Of everything in C, it looks most like a web dashboard.
2. The office is only a small window thumbnail in PLAN, so the "computers and Doom approaching" feel comes mostly in WATCH.

## E. Rooms and Stations -- about average; belongs here: yes
References:
- `diegetic/shots/game-dev-tycoon-1.jpg` and `game-dev-tycoon-2.jpg`: the same idea, rooms and staff you visit as the office grows. E's Lab Floor camera (place2) uses Pip's own sprites in the same register.
- `diegetic/shots/hypnospace-outlaw-1.jpg`: a fixed frame with places swapped underneath. E's top instrument bar and F1-F4 station tabs do this cleanly.
- `diegetic/shots/xcom-2-1.jpg`: a base you move around in, with a threat readout always on top.
- `reference-games/shots/football-manager-2024-1.jpg`: dense list-and-plan management. E's action list and CRT queue are as readable.

E is the only one of ours with the operator silhouette in PLAN, and the stations give the "more screens and flows" Alex asked for.

What holds it back:
1. The action list is a plain beige list with "+" and ">" buttons. It reads as a form, not as an object in the room.
2. The Lab Floor camera is a sparse flat box against a richly painted room, so the two art styles clash.
