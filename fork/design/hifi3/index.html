The preferred direction: H

The game is about heading towards a disaster you may not be able to stop. These mock-ups give its main screen a look to match: a dark, worn office, with paper as the only light thing on screen. The layout keeps what already works. Every instrument (P(Doom), reputation, Attention) uses one flat, quiet meter style, which keeps the doom bands' colours. As doom rises, the room behind the controls darkens, but the controls stay where they are.

H is the preferred direction. The desk faces the player. Menus open from a rack of plates on the right. The month's plan sits on the desk, and Commit the Month is a small button with the hours held for events beside it.

The spec for H

Read the spec

This spec describes concept H, the main game screen redrawn as a dark, worn desk. It is written for whoever builds it in Godot, and for Pip, who owns the game. The mock-up it describes is fork/design/hifi3/H/concept.html (open it with ?state=1 to ?state=6). The stills are H/s1.webp to H/s6.webp. The full working notes are H/notes.md.

Every number below was checked against godot/data/, the code or the ADRs, and the source is named. Where the mock-up differs from today's game, the text says so.

1. What H is for, and what it must not change

1.1 The aim

H keeps the player's view on one desk for the whole month. You plan at the desk, watch the month play out from it, and read the month's results on it.

  • The room behind the desk is Pip's office art. It gets darker as doom rises, so the player feels the danger before they read a number.
  • Everything the player acts on sits in panels that never darken. Doom is a layer over the room. It is not a repaint of the controls.
  • Paper is the only light thing on screen: the advisor's note, an event that needs a reply, and the month review. When paper is up, it holds the keys.
  • Each new month opens with the date typed across the top left, with a typewriter sound, like the title card of a spy film. It names only the date. The game names no place for the lab, so the dateline names none.
  • The look is grim and worn: near-black grounds, bone-coloured text, brass edges, film grain and grime. Anything that does not fit that character is left out. The mock-up's "LAB TERMINAL 1" label and its CRT scan lines were removed for that reason.

1.2 What it must not change

H is a UI and presentation change only.

  • No mechanics. With the same seed and the same inputs, a run must play out exactly as it does today. No cost, odds, formula, action, event or turn step changes. The UI draws no random numbers.
  • ADR-0015, no printed doom deltas. The P(Doom) meter shows the level the game already shows (for example 40.2%), its band name and a RISING label. Nowhere does H print a change in doom as a number. The month review reports doom by band only: "HIGH -> SEVERE (crossed up)".
  • ADR-0001, spending buys sight. H shows only what the player already sees today, or what can be worked out from it. Things the game hides stay hidden:
    • where doom comes from, unless the player has paid to see it;
    • rivals the player has not discovered (the review has no rivals block unless the game prints one);
    • a candidate's hidden self-worth and offer range;
    • the odds and results of an event choice before it is made;
    • what a play will yield before Commit. The plan's AFTER COSTS line subtracts the listed costs and nothing else.
  • ADR-0011, the Attention rule (amendment 2026-07-28):
    • The month's Attention is 20 hours on the standard difficulty (data/balance/defaults.json attention_per_month: 24 easy, 20 standard, 16 hard). planning_share 0.6 splits it into 12 planning hours and 8 operating hours.
    • Operating work may spill into planning hours. Planning work may never use operating hours. The gate is MonthPlan.can_spend_hours (scripts/core/month_plan.gd:182).
    • The one exception to the spill is conference travel: it uses operating hours only and may not spill (ADR-0011 amendment (e), lines 169-171).
    • The reserve is untyped: hours not spent on plays are held for events, and what events do not use evaporates at month end. There is no banking (ADR-0009 point 4).
    • The game books a play's declared hours when it is queued. Plays that charge themselves (the hiring pipeline and the like) also debit hours when they run, at Commit (game_manager.gd:715-717). MonthPlan's pool totals are the authority. The mock-up's spill tag on a row is its own estimate, made in queue order; the game does not record a spill per row.

2. Tokens

2.1 Canvas

  • The game's canvas is 1920x1080 (project.godot: canvas_items stretch, aspect expand). All positions here are on that canvas.
  • With expand, a wider window adds space. Anchor the left column (the dateline, the plan sheet, the terminal) to the left edge. Anchor the right column (the meters, the rack, the held panel, Commit) to the right edge. The room art fills the space between.
  • The mock-up scales a fixed 1920x1080 stage, so 1280x720 is the same layout at two-thirds size. It was checked at both sizes.

2.3 Doom colours and bands

H uses the game's own doom colours and bands from godot/autoload/theme_manager.gd. It adds no new thresholds.

  • Bands (DOOM_STATUS_BANDS): NOMINAL below 15, ELEVATED below 37, HIGH below 52, SEVERE below 67, EXTREME below 80, CATASTROPHIC below 92, TERMINAL above that. "Band N of 7" counts from NOMINAL = 1.
  • Colour (DOOM_STOPS, blended between stops): green to 15, yellow at 30, orange at 45, red at 60, dark crimson at 74, deep purple at 87, saturated dark purple at 100.
  • Arc and ticks: get_doom_stroke_color(), which lifts dark tiers to a luminance floor of 0.22.
  • Text (the % and the band name): the stroke colour lifted further, toward a pale tint of itself, until it reaches 5:1 on the panel. The game has no such step today (see section 7.2).

2.5 Type

The mock-up uses five sizes at 1920: 16, 18, 22, 28 and 40 px, with a 16 px floor.

The game sets sizes from one number. godot/theme/base_theme.tres sets default_font_size = 19, and ThemeManager.get_font_size() offers four steps from it (FONT_STEPS): small 15, body 19, header 27, title 35. Pip's CI rejects literal font sizes in scenes and scripts. Build H on those tokens:

2.6 Spacing and lines

  • Outer gutter: 56 px on the left, 40 px from the top for the meters (24 px for the dateline), 40 px from the right and bottom edges.
  • Gaps: 16 px between the two meters and between the sheet and the rack; 40 px between the rack and the held panel. Inside panels: 16 px side padding, 12 px between rows.
  • Rack plates: 46 px tall, 10 px apart.
  • Lines:
    • panel hairline 1 px (frame);
    • dividers 1 px (frame-hi);
    • corner ticks 2 px, 12 px long, in brass;
    • a plate's left edge 3 px (amber when lit);
    • focus is a 1 px amber hairline; the selected paper option has a 2 px ink frame.
  • Corners: 2 px radius on panels, 1 to 2 px on chips and keycaps. Nothing is rounded more than that.

2.7 Grime and texture

  • Panels: flat dark fill with soot on the inside (a 26 px inner shadow at 42% black, and a heavier one along the bottom edge). Over the fill: low-frequency smut and fine grain at 16% (screen blend), and Pip's tex_painted_metal_panel at 5% as scuffs. Worn brass ticks on the four corners.
  • Paper: the gradient above. Smut and grain multiplied at 16%, burnt edges (inner shadows), one fold crease, darker worn corners. The event card's fold sits at 36.5% of its height so it misses the text.
  • Whole screen: film grain at 20% (overlay blend).
  • Grime must never sit under small text at a strength that breaks the contrast floor. The mock-up's textures are procedural noise; the game can use small tiling textures instead.

2.8 The contrast floor

All text must reach 4.5:1 against what is really behind it.

H was measured on the rendered pixels, not on the CSS colours, so grain, grime, dim layers and antialiasing all count. The background is the median of the text's box. The text colour is the stroke core: the k-th pixel farthest from the background, where k = max(4, 0.5% of the box). Text under a sheet is skipped.

3. Components

Positions are x,y on the 1920x1080 canvas.

3.1 P(Doom) meter

  • Panel at 1400,40, 280x330.
  • A 270-degree arc (radius 96, stroke 16) in the doom stroke colour, with a tick at each band edge.
  • In the middle: the % at 40 px and the band name at 22 px (18 px when the name is long, like CATASTROPHIC). Both in the doom text colour.
  • Under the arc:
    • a RISING chip, when the game reports a rising trend (a label, never a number);
    • a 7-step band ladder with the current band marked;
    • the line "band N of 7". At band index 5 and up it becomes the game's own warning, "Very close to game over" (main_ui.gd:1010).
  • States: normal; rising; warning (band 5+). The panel itself never changes colour.

3.2 Reputation tachometer

  • Panel at 1696,40, 184x330.
  • A semicircle (radius 64, stroke 14) in bone #a99d88, scale 0 to 100, the value in mono under it.
  • A blood-red mark at 0 with the note "0 ends the run". That is the game's rule: reputation at 0 or below ends the run (game_state.gd:791; death_attribution.gd:46 files it as a reputation death).
  • No colour change by level. The game's own low-reputation warnings (30 and under, 20 and under; main_ui.gd:1015) stay in the game's log.

3.3 Dateline

  • Top left at 56,24. Courier Prime 700, 28 px, 5 px tracking, #d9d1bf, upper case, date only: "NOVEMBER 1, 2017".
  • A worn ribbon: letters land a little lighter or lower in a fixed pattern. Opacities 1, .86, .97, .9, 1, .82, .95, 1, .88 and offsets of 0 or 1 px, repeating. It is fixed, not random.
  • When it types: only when a month begins (the first screen, and after the month review). Inside a month it shows the date already set. During WATCH the day changes in place without typing ("NOVEMBER 14, 2017"), so it never disagrees with the clock.
  • Timing: the first letter at 260 ms (60 ms when the sound plays), then one letter every 95 ms (with the sound, +-22 ms per key); a space adds 60 ms and a comma adds a 40 ms beat. "NOVEMBER 1, 2017" types in about 1.6 s, and the carriage return stops about 0.56 s after the last letter. Drive the letters from the sound's schedule so they land on the strikes.
  • With reduced motion (if the game gets such a setting), show the date whole. The mock-up still plays the sound.
  • It sits above every dim layer, and menus open below it.

The sound (synthesised in the mock-up, between SOUND_START and SOUND_END in concept.html; nothing is sampled from a film). Aim: the hard typewriter strike of a spy-film title card: steel on the platen first, a short thud through the desk under it, no bell.

Each key strike is five layers. Three random values per key vary it: v (0.85 to 1.15, how hard), p (0.94 to 1.06, the noise layers' pitch) and q (0.975 to 1.025, the thump's own narrower pitch, so keys do not sound like tuned blocks).

  • Timing: 95 ms between keys, +-22 ms random. A space is silent and adds 60 ms. A comma adds a 40 ms beat after it.
  • Carriage return, 320 ms after the last key: a 240 ms slide (noise, low-pass sweeping 550 to 1750 Hz, rising to 0.27), then the stop (a sine falling 95 to 42 Hz over 120 ms at 1.0, decaying over 320 ms, plus a 300 Hz band-pass knock at 0.4 for 80 ms).
  • Room and level: a 0.7 s noise reverb tail, its wet path low-passed at 2.5 kHz, at 23% wet; a compressor (threshold -18 dB, ratio 4, attack 2 ms, release 120 ms); master gain 0.84.
  • One at a time. While a play is still sounding, through its room tail, a new call is ignored: it returns null and fires no callbacks. The control that started it stays disabled until onDone, with or without reduced motion. Two plays can never overlap. The game needs the same rule.

For the game asset, see section 6.3.

3.4 Menu rack

  • Panel at 1268,612, 276x428. Seven plates, 46 px tall, 10 px apart. Each plate has Pip's icon, a condensed caps name and a keycap.
  • PLAN set: ACTIONS A, PLAN P, STAFF E, UPGRADES U, LEDGER L, IN-TRAY R, GUIDE F1.
  • WATCH set: FEED F, STAFF E, LEDGER L, GUIDE F1 live; ACTIONS, PLAN and UPGRADES shown disabled.
  • States:
    • rest;
    • lit (the open menu): amber left edge and keycap;
    • hint: an amber ring and glow, used once, for the tutorial's first pointer;

3.5 Plan panel (the terminal)

  • Panel at 56,612, 560x428. On the desk it is the month at a glance:
    • "NOVEMBER PLAN";
    • the Attention bars, planning N of 12 and operating N of 8, one cell per hour, filled = hours left;
    • the queue in short;
    • when empty, "Plan is empty / open Actions [A]".
  • In WATCH the same panel plays the month back: the month and "workday N of 22", a bar with one segment per workday and today marked, then the feed (3.11).

3.6 Held for events, and Commit

  • Commit sits at 1584,980, 296x60, and never moves. It is a dark plate with an amber hairline and caps "COMMIT THE MONTH".
    • It shows the Enter keycap only when nothing above it owns Enter.
    • Waiting (under the month review): dimmed.
    • WATCH: "MONTH IN PLAY", dashed and inert.
  • Held for events is a small panel directly above Commit (1584, top 856, 296x112): "HELD FOR EVENTS 7 hr" and the line "untyped; what events do not use is gone at month end". 3.7 Action card file (Actions, A)

  • A dark sheet at 56,88, 1196x952 (E's card file, retextured). The rack and Commit stay in view.

  • A balance strip: money, compute, research (in warn colour with BELOW ZERO when under 0), papers, and hours left as pips.

  • A card is one of two kinds:

  • Card states:

3.8 Month plan table (Plan, P)

  • The same sheet position as the card file.
  • At the top, the Attention grid: 12 planning cells and 8 operating cells. Filled = left. A hatched planning cell is an hour that operating work took, with a one-line legend.
  • One row per queued play, in run order. Repeats get their own rows. Each row: number, play, where it lives, a pool chip with its hours, its cost, and ^ v x (move up, move down, remove).
  • The play whose hours spilled into planning carries a "SPILL 1" tag in the POOL column, and a SPILL line under the grid says how many operating hours were drawn from planning. The tag is the mock-up's queue-order estimate (section 1.2); MonthPlan's pool totals are the source of truth.
  • Below the rows:
    • the total: plays, plan + op hours, money + reputation;
    • AFTER COSTS: money and reputation after the listed costs, and the hours held. Results of plays are not included, because they land at Commit;
    • Research quality (research_quality_selector.gd), default Standard;
    • Undo Z, Clear C;
    • the reserve stepper (- and +), live while any hour is unspent;
    • "P Esc BACK TO DESK".
  • Commit shows no Enter while the sheet is open.

3.9 Paper: the event card and the month review

Paper is the topmost layer when it is up, and it owns Enter.

  • Under a WATCH event, no other keycap shows: not on the rack, not on the feed.
  • Under the month review, the rack keeps its letters, and Enter belongs to the review sheet alone (Commit shows no Enter).

Event card (668,96, 640 wide):

  • "ACROSS YOUR DESK", the event's name and its data text.
  • One row per option: the option key, the option text, its effect, and its cost.
  • The selected option has a 2 px ink frame, an amber edge and the Enter keycap.
  • A footer: money in hand and hours held for events.
  • An event cannot be dismissed with Esc. Today's game swallows Esc on events (#452).
  • Example from the data (core_events.json talent_recruitment):
    • Fast-Track Hiring: $25,000 + 1 held hr, +1 safety researcher;
    • Standard Hiring: $25,000 + 1 held hr, +1 safety researcher. Its data cost is $25,000 only. event_dialog.gd adds the event's Attention price to any handled option that declares none;
    • Decline Offer: free, no change.
  • The mock-up labels the options 1, 2, 3. The build should use the game's advertised letters, Q, W, E (dialog_keys.gd, NAVIGATION_AUDIT principle P3). The game still accepts numbers without showing them.

Month review (56,190, 1184x850), a teleprinter sheet:

  • "FROM THE TELEPRINTER" and the date;
  • the title and lede, as game_manager.gd writes them ("Month Review -- December 2017", "December 2017 begins.");
  • the game's Attention line;
  • what happened, in dated blocks: the plays at Commit with the game's result messages, then each event and its outcome, then the held hours ("7 hr held: 1 went to Talent Opportunity, 6 evaporated.");
  • LAST MONTH'S MOVEMENT: funds before -> after, staff before -> after, and doom by band only;
  • rivals only when the game prints them;
  • one button: "Begin planning December 2017" with Enter.

Behind the review the desk is dimmed and Commit is shown waiting.

3.10 WATCH clock and speed

Inside the grown held panel:

  • "THE MONTH", then the clock: "TUE 14 NOV" and "workday 10 of 22";
  • "PAUSED FOR YOU", a blood-red bar, shown when an event has paused playback;
  • a speed row: Pause, 1x, 2x, 4x. Today's speeds are 0.20, 0.10 and 0.05 s per day (screen_mode.gd:37);
  • HELD FOR EVENTS.

Today's pause button is a disabled stub (screen_mode.gd:101), but events already pause playback. In H, the Pause segment shows that state. A pause the player can press is outside this spec.

3.11 Feed

The feed is the WATCH body of the plan panel. Each line has the day number in mono, then the game's own message:

  • "11 plays run at Commit", then each play's result;
  • events marked [!] when they wait for a reply;
  • "at work: 1 safety researcher";
  • "Feed and results [F]" opens the full log.

H adds no wording of its own to results. While an event waits, the feed shows no keycap.

4.1 Key rules

  • Enter and Esc belong to the topmost layer only. Paper over a menu over the desk: only the top one shows Enter or Esc, and Commit shows Enter only when nothing else is open.
  • A menu's own letter closes it again (A opens and closes Actions).
  • Today's Esc contract applies to every menu (UI_ESCAPE_CONTRACT.md): Esc closes the topmost panel, and every panel has a visible way out. Events are the exception the game already makes.

6.3 The sound asset

  • Reference: fork/design/hifi3/H/dateline-sound.wav, rendered from the mock-up's own code (TypeSound.render, seed 1917) typing "NOVEMBER 1, 2017". Mono, 16-bit, 44,100 Hz, 3.29 s. Raw peak -2.48 dBFS, written with no gain or normalisation; RMS -24.1 dBFS; no clipped samples. 14 strikes (56 ms to 1688 ms) and the carriage-return stop at 2.25 s.
  • For the game, make it as one-shots, so any date can be typed and the letters stay in sync:
    • 6 to 8 key strikes, varied as in 3.3 (contact, slap, thump, rattle and escapement tick in each);
    • one carriage-return slide and stop;
    • WAV, mono, 16-bit, 44.1 kHz, raw peaks at or under -1.5 dBFS, room and compression baked in.
  • One dateline at a time: a new month's type-on never starts over one still sounding (3.3).
  • The game picks a strike per letter at the dateline's timing. It may reuse the reference render's timing for a fixed string. These picks are presentation only: they must not draw from the game's RNG (state.rng).
  • Bus: the game already has an SFX bus (bus 1 in default_bus_layout.tres), with an SFX volume setting (game_config.gd). Put it there. godot/assets/audio/sfx/ is empty today, so this would be the game's first sound effect.
  • It plays only when the dateline types (a month's first screen), never on in-month day changes.

7. Open rule questions, and bugs found on the way

7.1 Where the mock-up follows the ADR and today's UI differs

  • The Attention spill.
    • ADR-0011 (amendment (e)) and the core gate (MonthPlan.can_spend_hours) let operating work use planning hours.
    • Today's UI pre-check in main_ui.gd:1725-1741 refuses a play when its own pool is short ("Not enough Attention: need N operating hours"), before the core gate is asked.
    • H follows the ADR. Screen 3 shows the 9th operating hour taken from planning.
    • Making the UI agree changes which plays a player can queue, so Pip must rule on it.
  • Reserve.
    • ADR-0011 amendment (f) and MonthPlan.set_reserve make the reserve untyped and not capped at operating hours.
    • Today's UI disables the Reserve button when no operating hours are left (main_ui.gd:1277-1281, with a comment that says the opposite of the ADR).
    • H shows Reserve live while any hour is unspent.
    • This affects what a player can do, so it is Pip's call too.
  • The always-on resource bar goes. H drops today's top bar of balances: they show in the card file (A), and money and reputation in the Plan sheet. Whether the player should see them on the desk at all times is Pip's call.
  • The AFTER COSTS preview is new. It adds no hidden information (it subtracts listed costs), but Pip may not want a preview at all.
  • Fonts: small at 15 vs the 16 px floor, and the missing 22 step (2.5).
  • Keys: P and F are taken today, and the action-bar keys go (4.2).

7.2 Bugs and data gaps found (not fixed)

  • Office Maintenance does nothing. It costs $5,000 and 1 hour (data/actions/operations.json), actions.gd has no case for it, and its text says "Improves morale slightly". The action bar hides it (action_bar_renderer.gd:83), but the Operations submenu still offers it.
  • Empty costs in core.json. Advertise a Role and Work Your Connections have costs: {}. Their real costs are in Balance hiring (advertise $8,000 and 3 hours; connections 6 reputation and 2 hours), so any screen that reads costs from the action shows them as free.
  • Research below zero (-12 in November) has no warning.
  • "Turn N" counts workdays, not months. The unlock gates compare against the same count. Safety Audit needs turn_min 5 only, so it opens in the first week. Strategic needs turn_min 10 and reputation at least 30 (data/actions/core.json:113-116), so it opens in the first fortnight while reputation holds.
  • Unmapped icons. tour_offices, scout_read, scout_meetups and scout_shitpost have no entry in data/icon_mapping.json, so the game draws its magenta placeholder.
  • Doom text contrast. The stroke colour's 0.22 luminance floor leaves CATASTROPHIC text about 1.8:1 on dark panels (G's measurement). H lifts text to 5:1 and keeps the game colour on the arc.
  • Pause is a stub. The WATCH pause button is disabled; only events pause playback.
  • Event pictures are packed but unused (godot/assets/images/events/README.md).
  • No bundled fonts and no sound effects ship today (2.5, 6.3).
